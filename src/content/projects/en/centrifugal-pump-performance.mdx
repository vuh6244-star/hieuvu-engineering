## Challenge

A pump manufacturer wanted to **build the H-Q performance curve** of a new impeller through simulation — reducing test-rig iterations — and to assess **cavitation** risk at high flow rates.

## Approach

- Full model: suction pipe, 7-blade impeller, volute and discharge pipe.
- Rotating region with **MRF** for steady operating points; design point re-checked with **Rigid Body Motion** (true mesh motion).
- **Schnerr-Sauer** cavitation model to predict vapour regions at the blade leading edge.

## Outcome

The simulated H-Q curve over 7 operating points deviated by **less than 3%** from test data in the high-efficiency range. At 120% of design flow, vapour appeared at the suction-side leading edge — the basis for a recommended blade inlet-angle change.

## Lessons learned

MRF is good enough for performance curves, but **rotor–volute interaction** needs RBM to capture pressure pulsations. Choosing the right modelling fidelity balances accuracy and compute time.
