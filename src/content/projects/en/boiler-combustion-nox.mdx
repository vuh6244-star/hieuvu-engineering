## Challenge

The plant needed to lower **NOx** at the furnace exit without increasing unburnt carbon or making major hardware changes. The question: would redistributing **secondary air** between burner levels be enough?

## Approach

- **Geometry:** furnace, burner levels and flue-gas path up to the superheater, rebuilt from drawings in Siemens NX.
- **Physics:** Realizable k-ε turbulence, solid-fuel combustion with Lagrangian particles, **DOM thermal radiation**, thermal and fuel NOx formation models.
- **Verification:** model calibrated against the plant's **operating measurements** (flue-gas temperature, O₂ and NOx) before any scenario was assessed.
- **Scenarios:** 4 secondary-air distributions and burner angles.

## Outcome

Shifting secondary air to the upper level, creating a fuel-rich zone in the lower level, cut furnace-exit NOx by about **18%**. Flue-gas temperature ahead of the superheater stayed within design limits. The temperature field also showed the flame no longer impinging on the rear wall, unlike the current case.

## Lessons learned

A furnace model is only trustworthy once it **matches plant measurements first**. From a verified model, every option is assessed on the computer before field trials, which reduces trial and error on site.
