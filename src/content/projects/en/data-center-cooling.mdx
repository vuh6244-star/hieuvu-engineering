## Challenge

A server room under expansion showed **hot-spots** at the end of rack rows, with some inlet temperatures above the ASHRAE recommendation (27 °C). The owner needed the root cause and a fix before adding more IT load.

## Approach

- **120 racks** modelled as heat sources with power-matched airflow, 6 **CRAC** units and a raised floor with perforated tiles.
- ~9M-cell polyhedral mesh, Realizable k-ε turbulence, buoyancy included (Boussinesq).
- Three scenarios compared: as-is, **cold-aisle containment**, and containment + tuned tile open area.

## Outcome

The as-is case revealed hot air **recirculating** over the top and around the ends of the rows. Containment combined with tile tuning reduced the maximum rack inlet temperature by about **4.5 °C** and allowed a higher CRAC supply temperature — saving cooling energy.

## Lessons learned

In data centers, **airflow distribution through the raised floor** matters as much as total cooling capacity. CFD shows the path of hot air that point sensors simply cannot.
